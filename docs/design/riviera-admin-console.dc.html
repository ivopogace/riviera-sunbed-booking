What a rate change does

It changes the split on bookings taken from now on. Every payout-ledger entry already written keeps the commission it was accrued at, so history cannot be repriced — a venue’s past statements stay exactly as they were sent.

Why the operator can’t set it

Commission is the commercial agreement between the platform and the venue, not a venue setting. The operator console shows the rate so there is no mystery about the split — it just doesn’t offer a way to move it.

Percent in, basis points out

The rate is stored as whole basis points so the split stays exact-integer arithmetic. The field takes a percent because that is how the deal is described; the value actually saved is shown next to it.

Nothing was served that day, so nothing accrued. Runs are only created for days with bookings.

Why the day after, never before

Cancellations close the evening before, and a weather refund can only happen on the day itself. Waiting until the service day is over means every refund is still paid out of our own account — we never have to ask a venue for money back. That is the whole reason the timing looks like this.

What you still do by hand

Nothing, on a good day. A transfer that bounces — a wrong IBAN, a closed account — comes back here to be retried once the venue fixes its details. A venue with no EUR account on file is held and rolls into its next run rather than blocking anyone else’s money.

Everything is euros

Guests pay in euros, the ledger is in euros, venues are paid in euros. No conversion happens anywhere in the platform — a venue that wants lek converts at its own bank, on its own terms.
